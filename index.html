29% of your traffic goes to countries you don't sell to

Your live markets are Czechia, the EU and the United States. The UK is not one of them — so those 354 UK sessions, 7 cart adds and 4 checkouts were never capable of becoming orders. The US is the real anomaly now

The US is an active market with Wasabi Logistics rates and a free-shipping option. It is 60% of all traffic you can actually serve — 1,104 sessions — and it produced 5 cart adds and zero orders. Italy, on a twelfth of the traffic, produced 7 cart adds and 4 orders.

The breakdown says why: 496 of those US sessions arrive with no referrer at all and 115 come from ad-network frames and the unrecognised domains. Roughly 600 of 1,104 US sessions look non-human. The 62 from Instagram and 95 from Linktree are the real US audience, and they are small.

Reading the three drops

Why four orders had no session behind them

This was the open question. Clarity's click map of the product page answers it.

Most-clicked elements · product page · 2,022 taps over 1,826 views

●

The cookie banner is the most-clicked thing on your product page

Accept takes 303 clicks (15% of every tap on the page) and Decline takes 100. Together the consent banner absorbs a fifth of all interaction — more than Ingredients, Benefits and everything else combined.

Shopify's analytics only start a tracked session once analytics consent is granted. 1,826 people viewed the page and only 403 touched the banner at all — so for most visitors, and for everyone who clicks Decline, Shopify never records a session. They can still browse and still buy. The order lands; the session does not.

▲

One in four people who answer the banner say no

100 Decline against 303 Accept. That alone caps how much of your funnel is ever measurable — and it is why 4 of 9 real orders arrived with no visit trail, and why Shopify counts 5 tracked orders where 9 exist.

How far down the product page anyone gets

Clarity scroll map, 1,826 product-page views, last 30 days. The line is the share of visitors who reached each depth.

●

Half your visitors never pass the first quarter of the page

95% get to 10% of the page. By 15% only 66% remain, by 25% just 48%, and only 19% reach the halfway mark. The steepest fall — a third of everyone — happens between 10% and 15% depth, right after the hero and the consent banner.

Average active time on the product page is 25 seconds (GA4) and average scroll depth across the site is 30.6% (Clarity). Whatever is below the fold is, for most people, not being read.

▲

Nobody moves around the site

GA4 records 1.69 page views per user; Clarity records 1.24 pages per session. There is essentially no browsing — people land on one page, spend 20–37 seconds, and leave. The one page that holds attention is /pages/science at 53 seconds, more than double the product page.

That is worth acting on: the content that keeps people is not the content they land on.

13 of 36 people who add to cart then take it out again

That is a 36% removal rate, and it is the clearest new signal on this page. People who removed had already decided to buy something — then saw a number they did not like. Shipping cost and the total are revealed at the cart and checkout; that is where to look.

▲

278 sessions with dead clicks

Nearly one session in ten clicks something that does nothing. Rage clicks are almost zero, so this is not frustration with a broken button — more likely images, headings or badges that look tappable and are not. Worth 20 minutes in Clarity's recordings to find which element.

Two things GA4 turned up that nothing else did

▲

Old WooCommerce URLs are still taking traffic

/product/afterglow/ drew 102 views from 73 users with 40 seconds of engagement — more attention than the real product page gets. /science/, /ankle-mobility-routine/ and /recovery-optimization/ are still being hit too. These are old-site paths; either they are serving something, or people are landing on errors. Either way they should redirect to their Shopify equivalents.

◆

The checkout-step events are not instrumented

GA4's checkout journey shows 32 begin-checkout users but only 3 reaching add_shipping_info and 1 completing — while the purchase journey shows 7 purchases over the same window. Those cannot both be behaviour. The checkout-step events are firing for only a fraction of checkouts, so GA4 cannot yet tell you where inside checkout people leave. Turning on the full Google & YouTube channel checkout events would close that gap.

Known quirks in the figures above

◆

Session totals move by grouping

The same query returns 2,705 / 2,706 / 2,713 / 2,715 sessions depending on how it is grouped — Shopify counts a session once per dimension bucket. Everything here uses the source × device cut (2,715 raw, 2,612 cleaned) so the funnel always adds up.

◆

Checkouts above cart adds on two days

13 and 14 August each show more sessions reaching checkout than adding to cart. That is buy-now / express checkout skipping the cart step, not an error — but it means "cart adds" understates intent slightly.

◆

Two clocks

The Admin API reports order times in UTC; Shopify Analytics uses store-local CEST. Orders near midnight land on different days in the two systems. The ledger dates here are store-local, matching the charts.

◆

Password-page and preview sessions counted

41 sessions landed on /password and a handful on shopifypreview.com — your own theme previews. Small, and mostly outside Bratislava, so the cleaning does not catch them.
